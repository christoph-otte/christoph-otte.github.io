# Editorial Standards and Absolute No-Gos

Scientific publishing depends on trust. The following are non-negotiable standards that all authors must uphold. Violations will result in immediate rejection of the manuscript, and may be reported to the author's institution.

---

## Research Integrity

### Fabrication and Falsification

**Never** invent, alter, or selectively omit data.

- **Fabrication** means reporting data or results that were not collected or observed.
- **Falsification** means manipulating data, equipment, materials, figures, or processes to misrepresent results.

This includes:
- Removing inconvenient data points without scientific justification
- Enhancing or retouching images beyond what is scientifically acceptable
- Adjusting a figure to make a result look more significant than it is

If you manipulated a figure in any way (e.g., contrast adjustment, cropping), this must be disclosed in the methods or figure caption. The original raw data must be available on request.

### Plagiarism

All text must be your own, or clearly attributed by quotation and citation. Plagiarism includes:

- Copying text from a source without attribution
- Paraphrasing so closely that it is essentially copying
- Reusing your own previously published text without acknowledgment (self-plagiarism)
- Submitting work written in whole or in part by an AI language model without disclosure

We run all submissions through plagiarism detection software. A similarity score above a threshold will trigger a manual review.

> **Note on AI tools:** Using AI writing tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Copilot) for grammar correction or rephrasing is permitted. Generating substantial portions of the manuscript text using AI is not. If in doubt, disclose the tool use in the Methods or Acknowledgments.

## Authorship

### Who counts as an author?

Authorship is not an honorary title. All listed authors must have made a **genuine intellectual contribution** to the work. The standard criteria (adapted from ICMJE) are:

1. Substantial contribution to conception, design, data collection, analysis, or interpretation
2. Drafting or critically revising the article for intellectual content
3. Final approval of the version to be published
4. Accountability for the accuracy and integrity of the work

Meeting all four criteria is required for authorship.

### Ghost authorship

Including a person as author who did not contribute (e.g., a supervisor who only provided lab space, or a friend who did not participate) is **not acceptable**.

### Guest / gift authorship

Excluding a person who *did* contribute substantially is equally unacceptable.

### Acknowledgments vs. authorship

People who contributed in a supporting role — proofreading, technical assistance, providing materials — should be thanked in the Acknowledgments, not listed as authors.

## Duplicate Submission and Prior Publication

A manuscript submitted to this journal must:

- **Not be under simultaneous review** at any other journal or conference
- **Not have been previously published** in a peer-reviewed venue (including conference proceedings)

Preprints (on arXiv, bioRxiv, etc.) are explicitly permitted — posting a preprint does not constitute prior publication.

If your manuscript is based on a thesis, seminar paper, or project report, disclose this in your cover letter and ensure the work has not been formally published through your institution's repository in a way that constitutes prior publication.

## Ethical Standards for Research Involving Living Subjects

If your research involved **human participants**, you must:

- State in the Methods section that the study was approved by an ethics committee (Institutional Review Board / Ethics Board), including the approval number.
- Confirm that informed consent was obtained from participants.

If your research involved **animals**, you must:

- State that the study complied with relevant animal welfare regulations and was approved by the appropriate authority.

Work that required ethical approval but cannot demonstrate it will not be published.

---

## Conflicts of Interest

All financial and non-financial conflicts of interest must be disclosed. This includes:

- Funding sources for the research
- Paid consultancies or advisory roles related to the topic
- Personal relationships that could have influenced the work

A simple declaration is sufficient: *"The authors declare no conflicts of interest"* or a specific disclosure. Concealing a relevant conflict is considered misconduct.

## Summary Table

| Violation | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Data fabrication or falsification | Immediate rejection; institutional report |
| Plagiarism (text or figures) | Immediate rejection |
| Undisclosed AI-generated text | Rejection; authors asked to revise and resubmit with disclosure |
| Ghost or gift authorship | Manuscript returned; corrected author list required |
| Simultaneous submission to another journal | Immediate rejection |
| Missing ethics approval for human/animal research | Rejection; cannot be remedied after the fact |
| Concealed conflict of interest | Rejection or post-publication correction/retraction |

---

## Corrections and Retractions

If an error is discovered after publication, authors are expected to contact the editors promptly. We follow the guidelines of the [Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE)](https://publicationethics.org/) for handling corrections, retractions, and expressions of concern.

Honest errors honestly reported are handled constructively. Intentional misconduct is not.
