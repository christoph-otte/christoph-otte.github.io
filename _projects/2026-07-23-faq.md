# Frequently Asked Questions

---

## About Eligibility

### Who can submit?
Any student currently enrolled at a recognized institution of higher education — bachelor's, master's, or doctoral level — may submit as a corresponding author. Work done as part of a course, seminar, bachelor's thesis, or independent research project is all eligible.

### Can I submit work I did with my supervisor?
Yes. Faculty and researcher co-authorship is permitted. However, the **first and corresponding author must be a student**, and the student must be the primary contributor to the work.

### Can I submit if I have since graduated?
Yes, if the work was conducted while you were a student. Please mention this in your cover letter.

### I am from a non-English-speaking country. Is my English good enough?
We do not reject papers on the basis of language alone. If your science is sound, our editors will work with you on language improvements. We encourage you to use grammar tools (e.g., Grammarly, LanguageTool) before submission, and to ask a fluent speaker to proofread if possible.

## About the Submission

### What is the page/word limit?
There is no strict page limit. However, we recommend:
- Original research articles: **4,000–8,000 words** (excluding references)
- Review articles: up to **10,000 words**
- Short communications: **1,500–3,000 words**

Conciseness is valued. If in doubt, write less.

### Can I submit a figure-heavy or equation-heavy article?
Yes. The LaTeX template supports figures, tables, and mathematical equations. See the [LaTeX Template](latex-template.md) page for details.

### Must I use the LaTeX template?
Yes. LaTeX is required for all submissions. This ensures consistent formatting and allows us to produce a clean PDF. If you have never used LaTeX before, we recommend [Overleaf](https://www.overleaf.com/) — it runs in the browser without any installation.

### Can I submit in German / French / another language?
Currently, we publish in **English only**. We may expand to other languages in the future.

### Can I submit work that was previously a seminar paper or thesis chapter?
Yes, with caveats. Seminar papers and thesis chapters are usually not written in scientific article format and will need significant restructuring. Chapters from a *published* thesis may be submitted only if they have not already been formally published elsewhere (check your university's open-access policy). Please mention the origin of the work in your cover letter.

## About Peer Review

### How does peer review work?
We use **single-blind review**: reviewers know who the authors are; authors do not know who reviewed their work. Manuscripts are assessed by two reviewers, typically advanced students, recent graduates, or early-career researchers.

### How long does review take?
Typically **4–8 weeks** for the first round. Revision turnaround times depend on the extent of the required changes. We notify you if there are significant delays.

### Can I suggest reviewers?
Yes — this is encouraged. In your cover letter, you may suggest up to three potential reviewers (with name, affiliation, and email). We are not obligated to use them, and we will not assign reviewers with an obvious conflict of interest.

### What if I disagree with a reviewer's comment?
You are always welcome to respectfully disagree with a reviewer, provided you give a clear scientific justification. Your point-by-point response letter is the place to do this. Editors make the final decision.

## About Publication

### Is the journal open access?
Yes. All published articles are freely available online to anyone, without a paywall, under a [Creative Commons CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) license. This means anyone may share and adapt the work, provided they give appropriate credit.

### Are there article processing charges (APCs)?
No. We charge neither submission fees nor publication fees. The journal is run entirely by students on a volunteer basis.

### Will my article have a DOI?
Yes. Every accepted article receives a unique Digital Object Identifier (DOI), which makes it permanently citable.

### Can I post my article on arXiv or my institution's repository?
Yes. We encourage preprint posting on arXiv, bioRxiv, or institutional repositories, both before and after submission. Please add a note when the paper is formally published: *"Published in [Journal Name], [year], DOI: …"*

### Can I list this publication on my CV?
Absolutely. A peer-reviewed journal publication is a publication. We recommend listing it under a section like "Peer-reviewed publications" or "Student publications."

## Miscellaneous

### I found an error in my published article. What can I do?
Contact the editors immediately. Minor errors can be corrected with an erratum notice. Significant errors may require a more formal correction process.

### What is the difference between a correction, a retraction, and an expression of concern?
- **Correction (Erratum):** A specific, limited error in an otherwise valid paper is fixed.
- **Retraction:** The paper is withdrawn because the results are unreliable or the work cannot be substantiated.
- **Expression of concern:** There is a question about the integrity of the paper that is under investigation.

We follow the guidelines of the [Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE)](https://publicationethics.org/).
