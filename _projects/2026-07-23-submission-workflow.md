# Submission Workflow

This page describes the full lifecycle of a manuscript from initial submission to publication. We aim to keep the process transparent and supportive.

---

## Overview

```
Author submits manuscript
        ↓
Editorial desk check (format, scope, completeness)
        ↓
    ┌── Does not pass ──→ Returned to author with notes
    ↓
Assigned to editor + peer reviewers
        ↓
    Peer review (~4–8 weeks)
        ↓
Decision communicated to author:
    ├── Accept (rare at first submission)
    ├── Minor revisions  ──→ Author revises → Re-check
    ├── Major revisions  ──→ Author revises → Re-review
    └── Reject           ──→ Detailed feedback provided
        ↓
Final accepted manuscript prepared
        ↓
Typesetting / layout check
        ↓
Published online (open access)
```

---

## Step 1 — Prepare Your Submission

Before you submit, make sure you have:

- [ ] **Manuscript** as a PDF compiled from the LaTeX template (see [LaTeX Template](latex-template.md))
- [ ] **Source files** — your `.tex` file and all figures as separate files (`.pdf`, `.png`, or `.eps`, at ≥ 300 dpi)
- [ ] **Cover letter** (see below)
- [ ] All authors have reviewed and approved the final version
- [ ] No identifying information in the manuscript body (blind review — see below)

### Cover Letter

The cover letter is a short document (max. one page) addressed to the editors. Include:

1. Title of the manuscript
2. Type of submission (original research / review / short communication / theoretical)
3. Subject area / discipline
4. A brief (2–4 sentence) description of the work and why it is suitable for this journal
5. A statement that the work has not been published elsewhere and is not under review elsewhere
6. Names, affiliations, and email addresses of all authors (this is the only place author names appear)
7. Optional: suggested reviewers (with email and affiliation, no conflicts of interest)

---

## Step 2 — Blind Review Policy

We use **single-blind peer review**: reviewers know who the authors are, but authors do not know who reviewed their work.

To ensure a fair process, the manuscript itself must be **anonymized**:
- Remove author names and affiliations from the title page (they appear only in the cover letter)
- Do not include acknowledgments that identify the authors
- Avoid self-citations phrased as *"In our previous work [ref]…"* — rephrase as *"[Author et al., year] showed…"*

---

## Step 3 — Editorial Desk Check

After submission, the editors check:

- Is the manuscript within scope?
- Is the LaTeX template used correctly and the PDF complete?
- Are all required files present?
- Is the cover letter complete?
- Is there obvious plagiarism or an ethical issue?

This step takes approximately **1–2 weeks**. If something is missing, the manuscript is returned to the corresponding author with a note — not a rejection.

---

## Step 4 — Peer Review

Accepted manuscripts (past desk check) are assigned to an **academic editor** and **two peer reviewers**. Reviewers are typically advanced students, recent graduates, or early-career researchers with relevant expertise.

Reviewers assess:
- Scientific soundness and rigor
- Clarity of writing and presentation
- Appropriate use of the literature
- Validity of conclusions

**Expected timeline:** 4–8 weeks. You will receive a notification if there are significant delays.

---

## Step 5 — Decision and Revision

You will receive one of the following decisions:

| Decision | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Accept** | Manuscript is accepted as submitted (very rare at first submission) |
| **Minor revisions** | Small changes required; re-submission reviewed by editors only |
| **Major revisions** | Substantial changes required; re-submission goes back to reviewers |
| **Reject** | Manuscript cannot be accepted; detailed feedback is provided |

### Responding to Reviews

- Prepare a **point-by-point response letter**: quote each reviewer comment, then explain exactly what change you made (or why you respectfully disagree, with justification).
- Do not ignore any reviewer comment — even if you disagree, acknowledge and respond.
- Mark all changes in the manuscript (e.g., using a different text color or track changes).
- Re-submissions must include: revised manuscript + response letter.

**Revision deadline:** typically **8 weeks** from the decision. Extensions are available on request.

---

## Step 6 — Acceptance and Publication

Once accepted:

1. The editorial team performs a final **layout check** and may send minor formatting queries.
2. Authors receive **proofs** — please review carefully for any typesetting errors.
3. The article is published online as an open-access PDF with a **DOI**.
4. Authors receive a notification email with the publication link.

---

## Corresponding Author Responsibilities

The corresponding author is the single point of contact for editorial communication. They are responsible for:

- Communicating with all co-authors throughout the process
- Ensuring all co-authors have approved each submitted version
- Responding to editorial queries in a timely manner
- Notifying the editors of any errors discovered after publication (see corrections policy)

---

## Withdrawal

If you need to withdraw a submission, contact the editors as soon as possible. Withdrawal is straightforward before acceptance. After acceptance or publication, withdrawal is only possible in exceptional circumstances and requires editorial approval.
